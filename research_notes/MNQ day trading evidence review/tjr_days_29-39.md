## How to read these notes

- Time markers are the caption block the words sit in. Blocks are about 30 seconds long, so the words were spoken between that marker and the next one.
- Text in double quotes is verbatim from the captions. `[=word]` directly after a word is my correction of an obvious caption error. `...` marks words I left out.
- "He says" means it is in the captions. "My inference" or "my reading" means it is mine and he did not say it outright.
- He does not show futures or MNQ anywhere in these eleven lessons. His examples are the S&P 500 (a CFD in MetaTrader with a broker he calls "hanko trade"), GBP/USD ("GU"), GBP/JPY ("GJ") and gold. NASDAQ is named once, only as one of the markets a viewer could choose (day 29 [0:13:24]).
- No clock time and no time zone is given anywhere in days 29-39 for the New York open. He only says "the first hour and a half" of the New York Stock Exchange session.

## Day 30: Execution (Putting the Pieces Together, part 1)

File: `day30.txt`. Length: about 9 minutes (last marker 0:09:13), about 1,700 words.

Summary: A no-charts lesson that lists the five building blocks, says what each one tells you, and then lists four ways to combine them into an entry, from fewest to most confluences. The viewer is told to pick one of the four and write it into the trading plan as the execution plan.

### Rules and definitions stated

The five building blocks

- [0:01:01] "we have liquidity sleep [=sweep] we have breakup [=break of] structure we have order block we have fair value gaps and we have equilibrium"
- [0:01:01] Liquidity sweep: "liquidity sweeps show us the potential for orders to get filled"
- [0:01:32] "means a high or a low getting taken out we don't know whether it's confirmed yet"
- [0:01:32] Break of structure: "confirmation that orders were filled and we see a market structure shift"
- [0:01:32] Direction example: "if we're in an uptrend and we see a liquidity sweep and then a breaker [=break of] structure to the downside" then smart-money orders were filled "and we're going to go in the opposite direction".
- [0:02:03] Order block: "order blocks are the price range where orders were filled"
- [0:02:03] Where it is: "that move up that caused the liquidity sweep where orders were able to get filled" (for a sweep of highs).
- [0:02:03] Fair value gap: "Fair Value gaps are the price range with a lack of liquidity"
- [0:02:03] to [0:02:34] "it's usually that big candle that big imbalance candle"
- [0:02:34] Equilibrium: "it's just finding that discounted price between highs and lows"

The four entry combinations, in his order

1. Sweep plus break of structure.
   - [0:03:05] "first liquidity sweep combined with a break of structure"
   - [0:03:36] "we see confirmation that orders were filled enter right because that's going to be the top of the move"
   - My reading: entry is taken once the break of structure is confirmed. He does not say "candle close" in this lesson (he does in day 37 [0:02:07] and day 38 [0:05:39]).
2. Sweep, break of structure, then order block.
   - [0:03:36] Used when: "let's say we don't like the risk to reward that that gives us"
   - [0:03:36] The order block is "our first retracement tool that we always look for".
   - [0:04:08] Entry and stop: "we enter at the order block stops above the highs" (short example).
3. Sweep, break of structure, then fair value gap.
   - [0:04:39] "we see a liquidity Suite [=sweep] we see a break of structure and we see a fair value Gap get filled"
   - [0:04:39] "these don't have to be simultaneous"
   - [0:04:39] It can come after a missed order block: "you missed the order block entry price trends lower and then you see a fair value Gap that you enter off of"
   - [0:05:10] Entry trigger is given two ways in one sentence: "to get hit and a reaction off of it or it just for it to get hit for us to enter"
4. Sweep, break of structure, fair value gap or order block, plus equilibrium.
   - [0:05:40] "plus a fair value Gap or order block getting filled filled plus equilibrium on top of that for ultimate Confluence"
   - [0:05:40] "this is like a foolproof plan to enter if you are a beginner"
   - [0:06:11] The extra condition: "along with the confirmation of price being in a discount"

Ranking and choice

- [0:05:40] "the higher up you get on this list the more risky you get"
- [0:05:40] "these two are kind of equal this is the most safe this is the most risky"
- [0:05:40] "it goes like one most safe even most risky three most risky" (unclear in captions).
- [0:07:12] "you need to choose which one of these that you want to use"
- [0:06:42] "this is part of your execution plan"

What he calls a confluence and how many are needed

- He uses "confluence" for each building block that lines up at the same place, and "ultimate Confluence" [0:05:40] for all of them together. He gives no minimum number. The smallest set he allows an entry from is sweep plus break of structure (combination 1).

### Worked examples

None. "we're not getting into charts today" [0:00:00].

### Behaviour and process rules

My inference: combination 1 (sweep plus break of structure) is "most risky", combinations 2 and 3 (order block, fair value gap) are the "kind of equal" pair, and combination 4 is "most safe". Day 32 [0:05:10] supports the first part: sweep plus break of structure is "the like riskiest the lowest confluence".
- [0:02:34] "let me shrink my face for y'all so you can read this": the written cheat sheet is on screen.

### Unclear or contradictory

- "it goes like one most safe even most risky three most risky" [0:05:40] cannot be decoded from the captions.
