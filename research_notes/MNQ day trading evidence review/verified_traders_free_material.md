## 1. Who has a track record that a third party has actually verified, and how?

### Takeaway
Only a small subset of the named educators has anything a third party actually checked, and in every case the check is narrow: a single contest account for one year (Williams, Davey, Unger), an accountant's review of a defined period that critics say was an attestation rather than a full audit (Brandt 1981-2010), or an author's vetting for a book (Raschke, Brandt, reportedly Breitstein). Carver and Hougaard publish their own real-money results but nobody independent has signed them off; Steenbarger, Bellafiore, Chan, Grimes and Brooks have verifiable roles or no public record at all; ICT and TJR have nothing verified.

### Inferences
- A workable tiering for the report: (A) organiser-published real-money contest standings for a named year — Williams 1987, Davey 2005-07, Unger 2008-10 and 2012; (B) accountant-reviewed multi-year record commissioned by the trader — Brandt 1981-2010, with the audit/attestation wording disputed; (C) vetted by an author for a book, method undisclosed — Raschke (1992), Brandt (2020), Breitstein (reported); (D) verifiable professional role only — Carver, Steenbarger, Bellafiore, Chan, Breitstein; (E) self-published real-money reporting, unaudited — Carver's annual posts, Hougaard's Telegram and claimed audits; (F) nothing — Brooks, Grimes, ICT, TJR.
- None of the tier A-C verifications covers intraday trading of Nasdaq futures specifically, and none is current: the most recent contest result for the three "classic" names is 2012.
- Contest wins verify that a real account made that return in that year, not that the method is safe: Unger describes a ~47-48% drawdown and up to 20% risk on a single trade; Williams's other accounts reportedly lost in the same year; Davey's 2006 win was reportedly driven by one copper trade. Contest returns should not be read as evidence about position sizing.
- The contest organiser is a broker with a commercial interest and its archive reportedly contains errors, so even tier A is "organiser-reported", not audited.
- Ironically the two people with the least verification among the non-influencers (Grimes, Steenbarger) are the ones whose free material is most explicitly about evidence and process; their value rests on the checkability of what they teach rather than on a P&L claim.

## 2. For the verified (or partly verified) people: what free material exists, and what does it teach that is actionable for intraday index futures?

### Takeaway
The free material from the most-verified names is mostly about process and risk rather than intraday setups: Brandt publishes risk-per-trade and win-rate numbers, Carver publishes cost-versus-speed analysis, Steenbarger publishes worked risk-of-ruin arithmetic on trade frequency, and Unger gives away a book. Concrete intraday rule sets come from Raschke, Hougaard and Grimes (sections 3, 5 and 6), who sit lower on the verification scale.

### Cited Findings

**Peter Brandt (free: public blog, archived Factor reports, interviews on peterlbrandt.com)**
- Win rate: "long-term win rate going back to 1981 is just above 42%"; a 2016 update says around 42%; a later short piece says it "hovers around 50%". — [Member Q&A collection](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/a-collection-of-member-qas/); [Factor Update March 20, 2016](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/factor-update-march-20-2016/); [In 280 Characters or Less](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/evergreen-280-characters-less/)
- Payoff over win rate: "a better metric than the percent success rate of a pattern is its win/loss ratio value"; for head-and-shoulders trades he reports about $2.15 won per $1 lost. — [Member Q&A collection](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/a-collection-of-member-qas/)
- Risk per trade: in 2011 he said he should "generally not risk any more than 1 percent of capital per trade" and cuts risk in drawdowns; historical maximum "100 basis points", later lowered to 60; "60 or so basis points on each trade as a general rule". — [2011 Mercenary Traders interview (PDF)](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/03.16.2011-Mercenary-Traders-Interview-With-a-Trading-Legend-Pts-1-8.pdf); [In 280 Characters or Less](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/evergreen-280-characters-less/)
- Cutting size when cold: in a poor stretch in 2017 he "reduced size all the way down to 20 BPs" and at one point 30 bps per trade. — [Factor Update September 3, 2017 (PDF)](https://peterlbrandt.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Factor-Update-September-03-2017-2.pdf)

**Brett Steenbarger (free: TraderFeed blog, 2005-present)**
- Trade frequency and ruin arithmetic: with a $1,000,000 account, 50% win rate and winners 30% larger than losers, at roughly two trades a day the chance of hitting a $200,000 loss limit is about 0.4%; "If you overtrade and place 1000 trades in a year and your average winner is only 10% larger than your average loser, you have nearly a 64% chance of tapping out." Conclusion: "by taking the very best setups and fewer trades, we can ensure that we stay in the game". — [Taking Intelligent Risks (Oct 2015)](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/10/taking-intelligent-risks-how-to-stay-in.html)
- Overtrading and win rate: a hypothetical trader's 55% win rate falls to 45% once overtrading sets in; without daily loss limits and a time-out process the trader "can blow up in a short amount of time". — [Learning When to Not Trade (Mar 2009)](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2009/03/learning-when-to-not-trade.html)
- Definition: overtrading is "taking risk without a clear edge in our trade"; it "means that we trade outside the range of our ideas". — [Trading Psychology Challenges 7: Overtrading (Dec 2017)](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2017/12/trading-psychology-challenges-7.html); [Why We Overtrade (Jul 2017)](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2017/07/why-we-overtrade-and-why-we-miss.html)
- Regime change: for a rule-based system a period of bad results "probably means that market regimes have changed"; "a central skill to long-term success is recognizing when your edge is eroding and pulling back from risk taking". — [November 2015 archive](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/11/)
- When not to trade: identify triggers such as a bad early loss so they can be intercepted; "our job is not to make great trades; it's to have the wisdom and restraint to allow great trades to come to us". — [Overcoming the Triggers to Poor Trading (Dec 2017)](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2017/12/overcoming-triggers-to-poor-trading.html); [March 2025 archive](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2025/03/)

- The numerically specific, free, primary-source teachings that can be turned into testable risk practices are: Brandt's per-trade risk band (about 0.6-1.0% of capital, cut to 0.2-0.3% in a cold streak); Steenbarger's demonstration that trade count multiplies ruin risk when the edge per trade is thin; and Carver's finding that cost drag rises with speed. None of these is an entry setup.
- Brandt, Carver and Unger are not intraday index-futures traders, so their relevance to MNQ day trading is in sizing, cost and validation discipline, not in setups.
- Steenbarger's numbers are illustrative arithmetic, not empirical results; they are useful as a template for the trader's own Monte Carlo rather than as evidence.

### Gaps

The only time-of-day material found is Raschke's general emphasis on the opening range and the most liquid hours (section 5) and Hougaard's open-based strategies (section 6).
- Unger's actual method (reported elsewhere to involve intraday bias and breakout logic on index futures) could not be sourced from his free material by search.
- Breitstein's and Williams's concrete rules were not retrievable without opening the episodes.
- Bellafiore/SMB's free YouTube material and Chan's blog were not examined.

## 3. Adam Grimes' free "The Art and Science of Trading" course: syllabus and what it says about pullbacks, failure tests, breakouts and the evidence

### Takeaway
The course is free, hosted on Grimes's MarketLife site, and runs nine modules from chart reading through pullbacks, the Anti, failure tests, breakouts, pattern failures and psychology. ### Cited Findings
- Grimes's own post says the course is hosted at MarketLifeTrading.com and "will always be, completely free"; its module list runs Module 1 chart reading basics, Module 2 deeper chart reading, Module 3 market structure and price action, Module 4 pullbacks, Module 5 the "Anti" pattern, Module 6 failure tests, Module 7 breakouts, Module 8 pattern failures, Module 9 practical trading psychology. (The search tool flagged the page as old; an earlier post described about 20 modules before the structure was revised.) — [adamhgrimes.com, "A new book, The Art & Science of Trading"](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/new-book-taas-of-trading/); [earlier announcement](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/coming-soon-a-new-trading-course/); [revision post](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/completely-new-revised-trading-course-nearing-completion/)
- Forum users describe it as "an excellent and free market training series. - The course has homework/workbooks; Module 4 homework covers record keeping and charting by hand. — [Scribd, Module 4 Homework (unofficial upload)](https://www.scribd.com/document/625964613/Module-4-Homework)
- Paid products exist alongside the free course (TradeCraft, a pullback masterclass, an options course). — [MarketLife TradeCraft](https://www.marketlife.com/tradecraft); [MarketLife pullback page](https://marketlifetrading.herokuapp.com/pullback)

**Pullbacks**
- Blog: a simple pullback pattern has an edge for both buys and sells across stocks, futures and forex, but "it's not a huge edge". — [The pullback: a trade that works](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/pullback-trade-works/)
- A later post on his firm's pullback scan reports "a clear edge that is both economically and statistically significant" over five years of data, and says "if you're looking at things like relationships to moving averages you're already stacking the deck with quantitative tools that don't offer an edge". — [Five Years of Data: Proving the Real Edge in a Pullback Trading Tool](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/five-years-of-data-proving-the-real-edge-in-a-pullback-trading-tool/)
- Stop guideline for pullback trades: roughly "2-4 ATRs beyond the entry as a starting point". — [How to trade pullbacks](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/trade-pullbacks/)
- Higher-timeframe conflict: "if you're trading a pullback on a 15-minute, but the daily and weekly charts are counter to the 15-minute trend, the failure rate of the pullback increases significantly". — [Topstep summary of Grimes's approach](https://topstep.com/blog/going-deep-on-adam-grimes-approach)
- The Anti: Grimes says the name comes from Linda Raschke and that it "is essentially the first pullback following a trend change". — [Fundamental Trading Patterns](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/fundamental-trading-patterns/)

**Moving averages**
- Course text (unofficial PDF copy): a moving-average test "should not be taken as a successful test of a moving average; it is a successful test of a pullback tendency"; it warns "It is always possible to massage statistical data to give a desired answer". — [Scribd copy of course text](https://www.scribd.com/document/386293646/The-Art-and-Science-of-Trading-by-Adam-Grimes-pdf)
- Interview: "there's just no statistical edge to a moving average, and I know that people publish a lot of work showing the opposite". — [Trading with Rayner interview](https://www.tradingwithrayner.com/art-and-science-of-technical-analysis-with-adam-grimes/)
- The course notes that market data usually violates the t-test's assumptions of normality and independence. — [Scribd copy of course text](https://www.scribd.com/document/386293646/The-Art-and-Science-of-Trading-by-Adam-Grimes-pdf)

**Failure tests**
- Definition: a market presses past a previous pivot or support/resistance and then quickly reverses — "a variation of a failed breakout"; Topstep's summary describes it as a failed breakout that reverses back into the range within the same bar and notes Wyckoff called the distribution version an upthrust. — [The Failure Test](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/failure-test-2/); [Topstep summary](https://topstep.com/blog/going-deep-on-adam-grimes-approach)
- Entry and stop as published: "Look for a point where price trades above resistance and immediately fails back below on the same bar", enter short on that bar's close "with a stop just above the high of that bar"; alternatively enter on the following bar if it reverses, with the stop "at or just beyond the new high set on the movement". — [Trading Pattern: the Failure Test](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/trading-pattern-the-failure-test/)
- Rationale: markets "will naturally seek out levels where there may be orders clustered"; the pattern is clearly defined so "it's easier to make concrete rules for things like stop location and trade management". — [The Failure Test](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/failure-test-2/)
- Caution for stocks and indexes: do not enter at the bottom of a pattern, because breaking past that pivot "often puts markets at a point where they'd tend to consolidate and pull back". — [The Failure Test](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/failure-test-2/)

**Breakouts**
- "the first pullback following the break of the resistance is critical". — [How to trade Complex Consolidations](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/trade-complex-consolidations/) (the search summary did not name the page for this quote)
- Grimes argues patterns are not all equivalent and need separate testing. — [No, it's not all the same](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/not-all-the-same/)

**Independent testing**
- A third-party GitHub backtest lab built on his 2012 book reports that the evidence "does not support a universal, automatable edge for these rules". Hobbyist project, not peer reviewed. — [GitHub, Code-Pearl](https://github.com/Code-Pearl/backtesting_adam_grims_inspired_strategy)

### Inferences
- Grimes's stance is the most test-friendly of the group: a pullback tendency exists and is small; moving averages add nothing; failure tests have crisp, codable entry and stop rules. That makes the failure test (trade through a prior pivot, close back inside on the same bar, stop beyond that bar's extreme) and the pullback-after-impulse the two candidates from his material that could be written as MNQ test rules.
- His evidence is his own, largely on daily bars across asset classes; nothing retrieved shows results on intraday Nasdaq futures, and the only outside replication found was negative for mechanical application. The edge he claims is explicitly small, so costs and execution would dominate at intraday frequency.

### Gaps
- The current module list on the MarketLife site was not read directly; the nine-module list comes from an older post on his blog.
- No published statistics (win rate, expectancy, sample size) were found for failure tests or breakouts, and the pullback posts' actual numbers were not retrieved.
- Whether the course states that most breakouts fail was not confirmed; no such quote was found.
- Grimes's own trading record is unverified (section 1).

## 4. Kevin Davey's free material on strategy development: process and stated pass rates

### Takeaway
Davey's free articles lay out a gated pipeline — goals, idea, limited feasibility test, walk-forward, Monte Carlo, incubation, then real money — and he repeatedly says most ideas fail, but no numeric pass rate was found in any free source searched. The only numbers found are a count of survivors (50 walk-forward-passing strategies, about 20 behaving normally and uncorrelated) and incubation lengths (three to six months in his article; six to twelve in a third-party summary).

### Cited Findings
- Process order from one of his diagrams: trading idea, limited feasibility testing, walk-forward testing and optimization, Monte Carlo simulation, incubation, full-size real-money operation (the search tool rendered the diagram's box order ambiguously). In his article, "The fourth step in my strategy development was forward walking" and "The fifth step in developing my strategy was Monte Carlo testing." — [kjtradingsystems.com, "How I Achieved Over 100% Annually In the Futures Market" (PDF)](https://kjtradingsystems.com/100PercentReturns.pdf); [Building a trading strategy (PDF)](https://kjtradingsystems.com/BuildingTradingStrategy.pdf)
- Goals first: planning includes how much money you will risk; if it is less than 10%, "you can't realistically expect returns of 100%"; define a "walk away point", "the point at which your losses cause you to quit". — [100PercentReturns.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/100PercentReturns.pdf)
- Walk-forward: "chunks of historical data are optimized, and then the best parameter set is chosen and 'walked forward' on unseen data"; "all unseen data sets are combined to create a walkforward performance curve". — [Walkforward Testing](https://kjtradingsystems.com/walk.html); [Why Walkforward Testing Is So Important](https://kjtradingsystems.com/walkforward-testing-for-algorithmic-trading.html)
- Monte Carlo: "I took all my trades, throw them in a hat, and start pulling them out one at a time"; "history never repeats itself, so it is important to run random number simulations of expected strategy performance". — [100PercentReturns.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/100PercentReturns.pdf)
- Incubation: run the strategy live without trading it, "typically for a period of three to six months"; "this incubation period can reveal development mistakes and save significant capital". — [Building a trading strategy: After testing and optimization (PDF)](https://kjtradingsystems.com/WhatHappensAfterOptimization.pdf)
- A third-party summary gives incubation as "6 to 12 months" and says each gate has a binary pass/fail outcome so only a small fraction graduates. — [Financial Wisdom TV summary](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/building-winning-algorithmic-trading-systems-kevin-davey-s-complete-framework-for-trading-success)
- Attrition, qualitatively: the output "usually fills a big garbage bin" and getting one strategy per year "takes a lot of work". — [Algorithmic Advantage podcast 036](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about); [IBKR Campus repost](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/algo-advantage-036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about-process-in-algo-trading/)
- "You might be shocked how many strategies fall apart in real time"; even with 100 well-tested strategies he does not trade them all — those that underperform, diverge or break behaviour are discarded. — [kjtradingsystems.com, Spring 2025 page](https://kjtradingsystems.com/2025-05kevingpt.html)
- Survivor count: a Davey-authored page says he has 50 walk-forward-passing strategies, with around 20 operating normally and non-correlated. No denominator is given. — [kjtradingsystems.com walkforward article](https://kjtradingsystems.com/walkforward-testing-for-algorithmic-trading.html) (the search summary did not confirm which Davey page carried this)
- Position sizing warning: a Monte Carlo example shows that even a good system can blow up an account when position size is too large; one article uses a $5,000 account with $250 as the maximum loss per trade as an illustration. — [Don't Be a Gunslinger (PDF)](https://kjtradingsystems.com/Gunslinger.pdf) (page attribution not confirmed)
- Other free pieces on his site: a study of exits across 567,000 backtests, an article on high-win-rate systems, an intro to algo trading for retail traders, a trend-following article. — [What 567,000 Backtests Taught Me About Algo Trading Exits](https://kjtradingsystems.com/algo-trading-exits.html); [The Secrets to Achieving a 90% Wins](https://kjtradingsystems.com/winning-percentage-trading.html); [IntroAlgoTrading.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/IntroAlgoTrading.pdf); [Winning System.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/Winning%20System.pdf)

### Inferences
- The process itself is the transferable asset for a discretionary MNQ trader: write the setup as rules, do a small feasibility check on a slice of data, test on unseen data without re-tuning, shuffle the trade list to see the drawdown distribution, then paper-trade for months before sizing up. Each gate is pass/fail.
- Davey's contest returns came with a stated willingness to risk a large share of the account (his own "if it is less than 10% you can't expect 100%" framing), so the returns and the risk appetite are inseparable.
- The absence of a published pass rate in free material means any specific figure quoted elsewhere for Davey (for example "1 in 100") should be treated as unsourced until found in his book or an interview transcript.

- His exact pass criteria at each gate (for example the share of feasibility iterations that must be profitable, or Monte Carlo risk-of-ruin and return-to-drawdown thresholds) were not retrieved.
- Davey's view on prop-firm evaluation accounts and on small accounts was searched for twice and not found.
- The detailed findings of the 567,000-backtest exit study were not retrieved.

## 5. Linda Raschke's documented short-term setups: published rules and independent testing

### Takeaway
A primary copy of the Holy Grail rules is on Raschke's own site (a 1997 newsletter reprint), and secondary sources agree on the outline of the 80-20 and Turtle Soup rules, but the exact "Street Smarts" text was not retrieved and sources differ on trigger details. Independent tests found are few, informal and mostly weak; Raschke herself says she does not design fully specified trading systems.

### Cited Findings

**Her own framing and free library**
- Her site hosts a free library of articles, interviews, recommended books and videos, and a free first chapter of "Trading Sardines". — [lindaraschke.net](https://lindaraschke.net/); [Articles](https://lindaraschke.net/articles/); [Trading Sardines page](https://lindaraschke.net/trading-sardines/)
- In an Active Trader interview she says she "does not design or test full-blown trading systems as most people are used to thinking of them — specific sets of rules for entering, exiting and placing stops"; her work is pattern statistics and market tendencies that guide decisions. — [Active Trader interview, part 2 (PDF on her site)](https://lindaraschke.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/raschke_pt2_0304.pdf)
- Rule attributed to her: "Buy the first pullback after a new high. Sell the first rally after a new low." — [EliteTrader thread, "Linda Raschke's 12 Technical Trading Rules"](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/linda-raschkes-12-technical-trading-rules.294039/) (page attribution not confirmed); see also [TraderGav, 50 time-tested rules](https://tradergav.com/linda-bradford-time-tested-trading-rules/)
- She stresses trading during the most liquid times, "This means trading the opening range"; a secondary site says the first hour's range "establishes the framework for the rest of the trading day". — [MoneyShow video listing](https://moneyshow.com/video/11577); [Traders Mastermind](https://tradersmastermind.com/linda-raschke-trading-strategy/)
- A secondary site says she mostly traded S&P 500 futures, with positions "no more than 10-15 minutes" and three to four trades a day at one stage. Single secondary source. — [RoboForex blog](https://roboforex.com/blog/education/trading-strategies-that-were-a-revolution-three-strategies-of-linda-raschke/)

**Holy Grail (pullback in a strong trend)**
- Primary-hosted source: "The 14-period ADX must initially be greater than 30 and rising"; look for a retracement to the 20-period exponential moving average; enter with a buy stop above the prior bar; protective stop at the new swing low. — [AIQ Opening Bell, August 1997, reprinted on lindaraschke.net (PDF)](https://lindaraschke.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/august1997.pdf)
- A book summary lists the same: 14-period ADX above 30 and rising, pullback to the 20-period EMA, entry on a break of the prior bar's high or low. — [dbtech45 book summary](https://dbtech45.com/stacks/epdf-pub-street-smarts-high-probability-)
- Clarifications from secondary sources: reaching the EMA is not itself an entry; the original specifies an exponential, not simple, average (some retellings use a 20 SMA). Exit guidance reported second-hand: trail a stop below the 20 EMA or take profit at the recent swing high. — [EBC](https://www.ebc.com/forex/holy-grail-trading-setup); [Capital.com](https://capital.com/en-ae/learn/trading-strategies/holy-grail-trading-strategy); [Medium/Mudrex](https://medium.com/mudrex/the-holy-grail-trading-strategy-1668bacf99a2)

**80-20s (fade of a failed follow-through after a wide-range reversal day)**
- "Street Smarts" lists "80-20's" among its "test" setups alongside Turtle Soup, Turtle Soup Plus One and Momentum Pinball. — [Street Smarts table of contents (PDF copy)](https://thechartist.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/504/StreetSmarts(LaurenceConnors).pdf)
- Definition: a session "that opens in the top or bottom fifth of its high-low range and closes in the opposite fifth, then fades the next session if price pokes past that day's extreme and comes back through"; the numbers "are not a forecast that 80 percent of trades win". — [The Right Trader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trading-strategies/eighty-twenty-setup)
- Buy side as described in a magazine article: "Today, prices must go below the previous day's low at least five to 15 ticks (as a guideline)", with entry at yesterday's low once price comes back above it. — [Traders.com (Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities online)](https://technical.traders.com/tradersonline/display.asp?art=2527)
- A coded version: "Wait till today's Low breaks the previous day's one at least by 5 ticks", place a buy pending order at yesterday's low, initial stop at the day's low. — [MQL5 article](https://mql5.com/en/articles/2785)
- Sources disagree on whether the trigger is a resting stop order at the prior low or a discretionary entry on the move back through it.

**Turtle Soup (fade of a failed 20-day breakout)**
- Developed by Raschke with Connors in the 1990s as a counter to Turtle breakouts; setup is a new 20-day high or low where the prior extreme is at least three to four sessions old; Connors and Raschke "required the prior extreme to be at least four sessions old"; a next-day variant is Turtle Soup Plus One. — [LuxAlgo library](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/turtle-soup.md); [TradingView script description](https://www.tradingview.com/script/32Y7ZsaN-Grok-Claude-Turtle-Soup-Strategy)
- Regime dependence reported: "better in rotational markets, poor in persistent trends"; the reusable core is said to be the trap logic rather than the 20-day lookback. — [LuxAlgo library](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/turtle-soup.md)

**Anti**
- Described as entering on the first oscillator pullback after a trend change, tied to her 3/10 oscillator and its signal line; another summary describes it with stochastic %K/%D hooks. The two descriptions differ. — [MQL5 article on the 3/10 oscillator](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/24280); [SoBrief summary](https://sobrief.com/books/street-smarts)
- Grimes's gloss: "essentially the first pullback following a trend change". — [adamhgrimes.com, Fundamental Trading Patterns](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/fundamental-trading-patterns/)

**Independent testing**
- Oxford Strat publishes simulated portfolio tests of the Street Smarts setups (profit factor, Sharpe, CAGR, drawdown, win rate) with the standard disclaimer that simulated results do not represent actual trading; the numbers were not in the retrieved text. — [Oxford Strat](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=5614); [Oxford Strat](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=5513)
- A ProRealCode user testing Turtle Soup on forex reported "a bad profit factor, even if the strategy is profitable on the long term, with most forex pairs". — [ProRealCode](https://prorealcode.com/prorealtime-trading-strategies/turtle-soup)
- A hobbyist quant write-up of a Turtle Soup variant on EURUSD daily judged results likely poor, partly because sweep-and-reverse setups are rare on daily bars, and argued 100+ trades are needed for conclusions. — [quant-study-notes project](https://tamaraabu4-cell.github.io/quant-study-notes/projects/turtle-soup.html)
- A TradeStation coding blog implemented the Street Smarts setups. — [street-smarts-tradestation blog](http://street-smarts-tradestation.blogspot.com/2008_03_01_archive.html)

### Inferences
- Three Raschke/Connors setups reduce to two ideas that recur across this whole review: a pullback entry inside an established strong trend (Holy Grail; compare Grimes's pullback) and a fade of a failed push through an obvious prior extreme (Turtle Soup, 80-20; compare Grimes's failure test). The popular ICT/TJR "liquidity sweep" is the same failed-breakout idea under a different name; one source explicitly notes an "ICT variant" of Turtle Soup.
- The published setups are daily-bar patterns from a 1995 book. Applying them to intraday MNQ bars is an adaptation that the sources do not test; the few independent tests found are on other markets and are weak or mixed.
- Because Raschke herself says she does not trade fixed systems, the "exact rules" are best read as starting definitions for a test, not as a claim that the mechanical version made her money.

### Gaps
- The exact rule text of "Street Smarts" for all four setups (including tick offsets, order types, time-in-force, trailing-stop and exit rules) was not retrieved from the book; the Turtle Soup entry and stop rules in particular are only partly sourced here.
- No opening-range rule set of hers was found, nor her Taylor-method material, despite a targeted search of her site.
- No rigorous, published, independent backtest of any of these setups on index futures (daily or intraday) was found; Oxford Strat's figures were not readable.
- The original statistics Connors and Raschke give in the book were not retrieved.

- Verification status: see section 1 (claims audit and journalist review; nothing confirmed).

### Inferences
- The testable element is the position-management asymmetry: scale in only when the trade is in profit, never when it is offside, and take every stop. On MNQ that can be tested as "add one unit after price moves X in favour, with the combined stop moved so total risk does not exceed the original", versus a single-entry baseline. The sources do not give Hougaard's exact add rules, so the parameters would have to be the trader's own.
- His published open strategy (9-point stop, 6-point target on the DAX) has a payoff below 1:1 and therefore needs a win rate above 60% before costs — the opposite profile from his "let winners run and add" discretionary message. The two should not be conflated.
- His close, long-standing relationship with a spread-betting/CFD broker is a commercial tie worth stating next to any results claim.

### Gaps
- The exact add-to-winner rules (trigger, size, stop adjustment), his own win rate and payoff ratio, and his number of trades per day were not retrieved.
- The exact School Run rules were not read from an official source.
- No published broker statements, no named auditor and no named competition were found. The broker-data statistics he cites in the book were not confirmed; the closest matching public study is DailyFX's, which is not his.
- Everything here is DAX/Dow-centric; nothing specific to Nasdaq futures was found.

## 8. What the verified traders consistently say about win rate versus payoff, recency as a guide, and small or prop-firm accounts

### Takeaway
On win rate versus payoff the sources agree: a win rate around or below 50% is normal for profitable traders and the size of wins against losses is what matters, with retail broker data showing the typical losing pattern is the reverse. On recency, the evidence is that short windows are nearly uninformative (Brandt's win rate ranged from under 20% to about 50% across windows against a 42% long-run figure) and that validation needs months of unseen data. On small and prop-firm accounts, little was found from the verified traders themselves; what was found is about cost drag, undercapitalisation and the simulated nature of funded accounts.

### Cited Findings

**Win rate versus payoff**
- Brandt: long-run win rate "just above 42%"; "a better metric than the percent success rate of a pattern is its win/loss ratio value". — [peterlbrandt.com Member Q&A](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/a-collection-of-member-qas/)
- Hougaard: winning is "less about winning more often than about losing better"; add to winners, never to losers. — [Everand listing](https://www.everand.com/book/575704421/Best-Loser-Wins-Why-Normal-Thinking-Never-Wins-the-Trading-Game-written-by-a-high-stake-day-trader); [Goodreads quotes](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/96006358)
- Steenbarger: at a 50% win rate, whether the average winner is 30% or only 10% larger than the average loser, combined with trade count, moves the chance of hitting a loss limit from about 0.4% to nearly 64% in his illustration. — [TraderFeed, Taking Intelligent Risks](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/10/taking-intelligent-risks-how-to-stay-in.html)
- Broker data: across about 43 million retail FX trades, traders won more than half the time but lost over 70% more on losers than they made on winners. — [Nasdaq.com reprint of DailyFX](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/why-do-many-forex-traders-lose-money-here-number-1-mistake-2016-12-07)
- Davey has a free article on high-win-rate systems titled around achieving 90% wins, and another whose opening reads "Reward vs. risk? You can throw it out on the window"; contents not retrieved. — [kjtradingsystems.com](https://kjtradingsystems.com/winning-percentage-trading.html); [AvoidTradingParalysis.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/AvoidTradingParalysis.pdf)
- The 80-20 name is not a win-rate claim: the numbers "are not a forecast that 80 percent of trades win". — [The Right Trader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trading-strategies/eighty-twenty-setup)

**Recency ("the last few weeks") as a guide to what works**
- Brandt's short-window win rates: 32% over a few months in one account and below 20% in another, against a 42% long-run rate; he responded by cutting risk to 20-30 bps per trade rather than changing method. — [Factor Update September 3, 2017 (PDF)](https://peterlbrandt.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Factor-Update-September-03-2017-2.pdf); [Factor Update February 5, 2017 (PDF)](https://peterlbrandt.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/Factor-Update-February-5-2017.pdf)
- Davey: test on unseen data and never revisit in-sample parameters; then incubate for "three to six months" before real money; "You might be shocked how many strategies fall apart in real time". — [WhatHappensAfterOptimization.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/WhatHappensAfterOptimization.pdf); [Spring 2025 page](https://kjtradingsystems.com/2025-05kevingpt.html)
- Carver: after eleven years of live trading his "worst ever year" came in year 11, and in year 12 he was the worst of three benchmarks while still positive; he tracks live results against the backtest annually. — [April 2025 archive](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2025/04); [Year 12 post](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2026/04/annual-performance-update-year-12.html)
- Steenbarger: for a rule-based system a run of bad results "probably means that market regimes have changed", and a central skill is "recognizing when your edge is eroding and pulling back from risk taking". — [TraderFeed, November 2015](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/11/)
- Grimes: "It is always possible to massage statistical data to give a desired answer". — [Scribd copy of course text](https://www.scribd.com/document/386293646/The-Art-and-Science-of-Trading-by-Adam-Grimes-pdf)
- Sample size: a quant write-up argues 100+ trades are needed for meaningful conclusions. — [quant-study-notes project](https://tamaraabu4-cell.github.io/quant-study-notes/projects/turtle-soup.html)

**Small accounts and prop-firm accounts**
- Carver: micro futures "are a lot cheaper and more liquid" than before, but insufficient capital remains a problem with no easy fix; costs are one reason most retail FX traders lose. — [May 2021 archive](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/05/); [July 2021 archive](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/07/)
- Davey: if you are willing to risk less than 10% you "can't realistically expect returns of 100%"; oversized positions can blow up an account even with a good system. — [100PercentReturns.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/100PercentReturns.pdf); [Gunslinger.pdf](https://kjtradingsystems.com/Gunslinger.pdf)
- Contest evidence on small accounts: the World Cup minimum is $10,000 (annual futures) and a quarterly day-trading division is reported with a $2,500 minimum; Unger's 2008 win involved a ~47-48% drawdown. — [NexusFi listing](https://nexusfi.com/d/education/world-cup-trading-championships/); [CTE history page](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=235281); [Unger Academy](https://ungeracademy.com/blog/my-journey-to-winning-the-trading-championships)
- Prop evaluations (not from the verified traders): drawdown rules, not the profit target, are usually what fails accounts; retail evaluation pass rates are put at around 10% or lower by one UK analysis; a prop-firm CEO called the consistency rule "a payout trap"; reports of platform outages and a retroactive rule change. — [investingLive](https://www.investinglive.com/news/how-to-spot-unfair-prop-firm-practices-before-you-sign-up-20251224); [Freedom Isn't Free (UK)](https://freedomisntfree.co.uk/articles/prop-trading-uk); [Finance Magnates interview](https://www.financemagnates.com/executives/interview/fundedhive-prop-firm-ceo-calls-consistency-rule-a-payout-trap/)
- Hougaard on relative performance: "If 80% lose, then the competition isn't very hard to beat." — [Trading Manual (PDF)](https://www.tradertom.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Tom_Hougaard_The_Trading_Manual_Singles.pdf)

### Inferences
- Consistent across Brandt, Hougaard, Steenbarger and the broker data: judge a method by expectancy (win rate times average win minus loss rate times average loss), expect to lose on roughly half or more of trades, and treat "high win rate" marketing as a warning sign unless the payoff ratio is stated.
- A few weeks of results is inside the noise for every trader here with a long record: the practical rule is to respond to a cold streak by cutting size (Brandt, Steenbarger), not by switching setups, and to require months of out-of-sample or paper results before believing a new setup (Davey).
- Trailing-drawdown prop accounts interact badly with the profile the verified traders describe (sub-50% win rates, losing streaks, occasional large winners): a normal losing streak can end the account before the edge shows. This is an inference from combining the sources; none of the verified traders was found saying it.
- For MNQ specifically, Carver's cost-versus-speed work implies the per-trade edge needed rises with trade frequency; a discretionary day trader should measure round-trip cost (commission plus spread/slippage) as a fraction of average trade, which no influencer source reviewed here does.

### Gaps
- No direct statement about prop-firm evaluation accounts was found from Davey, Raschke, Brandt, Carver, Unger or Williams, despite targeted searches.
- No verified trader was found giving a specific minimum account size for day trading micro index futures.
- Raschke's and Williams's views on win rate versus payoff were not retrieved.
- Carver's direct opinion of discretionary day trading was not found; only his cost and speed analysis for systematic rules.
- A single broker-published page claimed the US $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum was removed on 4 June 2026; this was not verified and in any case concerns equities margin accounts, not futures. — [VT Markets](https://www.vtmarkets.com/discover/day-trading-is-it-realistic-for-someone-with-a-small-account/)
